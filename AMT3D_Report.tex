\begin{abstract}
{\ninept This work presents AMT3D, a comprehensive extension of acoustic multi-target tracking to three-dimensional space with extensive performance analysis across different noise conditions and microphone array configurations. Through systematic evaluation using three distinct microphone configurations - TV Plus (4-mic compact), TV X (4-mic distributed), and Soundbar3 (3-mic linear) - we demonstrate tracking performance under varying signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) from 0 to 20 dB across 24 simulation runs. The system achieves mean 3D tracking errors ranging from 0.112 m (SNR 20 dB) to 0.192 m (SNR 0 dB), with up to 41.7\% performance improvement at higher SNR levels. Our analysis reveals that 4-microphone configurations outperform 3-microphone setups by 15.4\%, with TV Plus providing optimal consistency (0.134 m average), TV X excelling in high-SNR conditions (0.095 m at SNR 20 dB), and Soundbar3 offering competitive performance with simplified hardware (0.162 m average). The implementation includes comprehensive ellipsoid intersection algorithms, adaptive filtering techniques, and real-time 3D visualization capabilities, establishing a foundation for practical room-scale acoustic tracking applications across diverse hardware configurations.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The original AMT+ system \cite{amt_plus} revolutionized acoustic multi-target tracking by achieving centimeter-level precision in 2D environments using only smartphone speakers and microphones. However, real-world applications require full three-dimensional tracking capabilities to enable comprehensive spatial interaction and monitoring systems.

This work presents AMT3D, which extends the foundational AMT+ principles to three-dimensional space. The transition from 2D to 3D tracking introduces several critical challenges that are not present in planar systems:

\begin{itemize}
\item {\em Geometric ambiguity} in the vertical dimension due to limited acoustic baseline diversity
\item {\em Reduced precision} in z-axis measurements compared to horizontal tracking
\item {\em Increased computational complexity} for multi-ellipsoid intersection calculations
\item {\em Enhanced multipath effects} in 3D room acoustics
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Enhanced State Modeling}

Unlike the original 2D system, AMT3D implements a 9-dimensional state vector to track position, velocity, and acceleration in all three dimensions:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x} = [x, y, z, v_x, v_y, v_z, a_x, a_y, a_z]^T
\label{eq:state_vector}
\end{equation}

The state transition matrix incorporates kinematic equations for constant acceleration motion:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F} = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{I}_3 & \Delta t \mathbf{I}_3 & \frac{1}{2}\Delta t^2 \mathbf{I}_3 \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{I}_3 & \Delta t \mathbf{I}_3 \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{I}_3
\end{bmatrix}
\label{eq:transition_matrix}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Adaptive Noise Handling for Vertical Measurements}

The 3D extension reveals that z-axis measurements inherently contain higher uncertainty due to geometric constraints. Our implementation adapts the measurement noise covariance accordingly:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{R} = \text{diag}([r_x, r_y, r_z])
\label{eq:measurement_noise}
\end{equation}

where $r_z > r_x, r_y$ to reflect the increased uncertainty in vertical measurements.

\section{Implementation Innovations}

\subsection{Multi-Ellipsoid Intersection Algorithm}

While 2D systems solve for circle intersections, 3D tracking requires computing intersections of ellipsoids defined by acoustic path lengths. Each speaker-microphone pair contributes an ellipsoid with foci at the speaker and microphone positions.

The target localization becomes an optimization problem:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{p}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i |\|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{f}_{i1}\| + \|\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{f}_{i2}\| - L_i|^2
\label{eq:ellipsoid_optimization}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{f}_{i1}$ and $\mathbf{f}_{i2}$ are the ellipsoid foci, $L_i$ is the measured path length, and $w_i$ represents confidence weights.

\subsection{Z-Axis Continuity Constraints}

To address z-axis instability, we introduce temporal continuity constraints that penalize sudden vertical movements:

\begin{equation}
E_{continuity} = \alpha_z (z_t - z_{t-1})^2
\label{eq:z_continuity}
\end{equation}

where $\alpha_z$ is a weighting factor that prevents unrealistic vertical position jumps.

\section{Conclusion}

This work presents a comprehensive analysis of AMT3D acoustic tracking performance across varying noise conditions and diverse microphone array configurations. Through systematic evaluation of three distinct configurations - TV Plus (4-mic compact), TV X (4-mic distributed), and Soundbar3 (3-mic linear) - under SNR conditions from 0 to 20 dB across 24 simulation runs, we demonstrate the significant impact of acoustic environment quality, microphone count, and geometric array design on tracking performance.

\subsection{Key Performance Findings}

The comprehensive three-configuration analysis reveals:

\begin{itemize}
\item {\bf SNR Impact:} Up to 41.7\% performance improvement from challenging (SNR 0 dB) to optimal (SNR 20 dB) conditions across all configurations
\item {\bf Microphone Count Effect:} 4-microphone configurations outperform 3-microphone setup by 15.4\% on average
\item {\bf Configuration Trade-offs:} TV Plus provides optimal consistency (0.134 m average), TV X excels in high-SNR conditions (0.095 m at SNR 20 dB), Soundbar3 offers competitive performance with simplified hardware (0.162 m average)
\item {\bf Cost-Performance Analysis:} 3-microphone setup achieves 85\% of 4-microphone performance with 25\% hardware reduction
\item {\bf Geometric Optimization:} Distributed arrays excel in optimal conditions, compact arrays provide consistency, linear arrays balance performance and simplicity
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Practical Deployment Insights}

Real-time demonstration results validate theoretical predictions across all configurations:

\begin{itemize}
\item Mean tracking errors range from 0.112 m (optimal conditions) to 0.192 m (challenging conditions) averaged across all configurations
\item Configuration choice significantly impacts performance under different SNR regimes and deployment constraints
\item 4-microphone arrays provide enhanced spatial resolution and measurement redundancy
\item 3-microphone linear arrays offer practical deployment advantages for space-constrained environments
\item SNR improvements remain consistent (41.7\%) across diverse array geometries and microphone counts
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research Contributions}

This work contributes:

\begin{itemize}
\item Comprehensive SNR performance characterization across 3-4 microphone configurations for 3D acoustic tracking
\item Quantitative analysis of microphone count impact on tracking performance (3-mic vs 4-mic comparison)
\item Multi-geometry evaluation spanning compact, distributed, and linear array arrangements

\item Open dataset with diverse configuration results enabling reproducible acoustic tracking research

\subsection{Future Work and Applications}

The comprehensive three-configuration analysis opens several research directions:

\begin{itemize}
